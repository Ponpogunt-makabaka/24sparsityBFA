## 4.2.2 METAttack Across Architectures and Datasets

To evaluate the generality of METAttack beyond the ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 baseline, we extend the attack to five representative architectures spanning CNNs and Vision Transformers, three datasets of increasing complexity, and two metadata encoding formats (position encoding and bitmask encoding). All models are first trained with 2:4 structured sparsity and then quantized to INT8 precision prior to attack. We apply a uniform physical budget of 50 bit flips across all experiments, with position encoding consuming 1 flip per attack iteration and bitmask encoding consuming 2 flips per iteration (cost-2 swap). Table 2 summarizes the results.

**Table 2: METAttack results on INT8-quantized 2:4 sparse models across architectures and datasets. Physical budget = 50 bit flips for all experiments. "Early Stop" indicates the iteration at which accuracy first drops below 10%.**

| Model | Dataset | Classes | Init. Acc (%) | Pos. Enc. Final (%) | Bitmask Enc. Final (%) | Pos. Enc. Drop | Bitmask Drop |
|:------|:--------|--------:|--------------:|--------------------:|-----------------------:|---------------:|-------------:|
| ResNet-20 | CIFAR-10 | 10 | 92.70 | 11.10 | 44.80 | 81.60 | 47.90 |
| ResNet-32 | CIFAR-100 | 100 | 69.05 | 13.11 | 26.77 | 55.94 | 42.28 |
| VGG-16-BN | CIFAR-100 | 100 | 74.04 | 45.34 | 53.98 | 28.70 | 20.06 |
| MobileNetV2 | Tiny ImageNet | 200 | 54.20 | 4.73 (iter 3) | 6.82 (iter 3) | 49.47 | 47.38 |
| DeiT-Tiny | Tiny ImageNet | 200 | 54.23 | 9.35 (iter 9) | 9.56 (iter 8) | 44.88 | 44.67 |

Several key observations emerge from these results.

**Position encoding is consistently more vulnerable than bitmask encoding.** Across all five model–dataset combinations, the position encoding metadata format suffers a larger accuracy degradation than its bitmask counterpart under the same physical budget. This is expected: each position encoding flip costs only 1 physical bit flip while achieving a full pattern transition, whereas bitmask encoding requires a cost-2 swap (one bit on, one bit off) to maintain the popcount-2 validity constraint, effectively halving the number of attack iterations. For instance, on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10, position encoding drives the model to 11.1% top-1 accuracy (near the 10% random baseline), while bitmask encoding reaches only 44.8% under the same 50-flip budget.

**Architectural vulnerability varies significantly.** MobileNetV2 is the most vulnerable architecture: on Tiny ImageNet, merely 3 attack iterations (3 physical flips for position encoding, 6 for bitmask) are sufficient to collapse accuracy below 10%. This extreme fragility likely stems from MobileNetV2's narrow pointwise bottleneck structure, where a small number of critical sparse patterns disproportionately govern feature propagation. In contrast, VGG-16-BN exhibits the greatest resilience, retaining 45.34% accuracy even after the full 50-flip budget under position encoding. VGG's dense, highly redundant convolutional layers provide inherent tolerance to metadata perturbations. DeiT-Tiny shows intermediate vulnerability, requiring 9 iterations (position encoding) to reach the random baseline—notably, the Vision Transformer's multi-head self-attention mechanism, while robust under FP32 attacks in prior experiments, becomes significantly more vulnerable once INT8 quantization is applied.

**INT8 quantization amplifies METAttack effectiveness.** Comparing with FP32 metadata attacks on the same models (where DeiT-Tiny on CIFAR-100 dropped only 16.47% after 50 flips), the INT8-quantized counterparts show dramatically larger accuracy degradation. This amplification arises because INT8 quantization constrains each weight to discrete levels (scale × {−127, …, 127}), causing even small pattern relocations to produce larger effective weight perturbations in the dequantized domain. The discrete quantization grid eliminates the fine-grained compensation that FP32 precision affords.

**METAttack scales across dataset complexity.** The attack remains effective when scaling from 10-class (CIFAR-10) to 100-class (CIFAR-100) and 200-class (Tiny ImageNet) tasks. On CIFAR-100, both ResNet-32 and VGG-16-BN suffer meaningful accuracy loss, with ResNet-32 approaching the random-guess level under position encoding. On Tiny ImageNet, both MobileNetV2 and DeiT-Tiny are driven below 10% accuracy, demonstrating that METAttack poses a realistic threat even for larger-scale classification tasks with hundreds of semantic categories.

In summary, these results confirm that the metadata vulnerability identified in 2:4 structured sparse models is not limited to a single architecture or dataset. METAttack generalizes across diverse CNN families (ResNet, VGG, MobileNet) and Vision Transformers (DeiT), across dataset scales from CIFAR-10 to Tiny ImageNet-200, and under both position encoding and bitmask encoding metadata formats. The combination of INT8 quantization with 2:4 structured sparsity—a deployment configuration increasingly adopted in production inference pipelines—creates a particularly susceptible attack surface that demands dedicated countermeasures beyond traditional BFA defenses.
